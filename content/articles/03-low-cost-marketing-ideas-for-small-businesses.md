Marketing doesn't have to mean expensive billboards or radio adverts. For most small and local businesses, the most effective marketing is simple, consistent and personal. Here are twelve ideas you can start using this week.

## 1. Make your business easy to find online

When people need a service, they search for it. Make sure your business appears with complete, accurate information on free platforms:

- A detailed Findora business profile with your services, photos and contact details.
- Your Google Business Profile, so you appear on Google Maps.
- A WhatsApp Business profile with your hours, address and catalogue.

Use the same business name, phone number and address everywhere. Consistency builds trust with both customers and search engines.

## 2. Ask happy customers for referrals

Word of mouth remains the most powerful form of marketing in South Africa. After a job goes well, simply ask: "If you know anyone else who needs this service, would you mind passing on my number?" Many people are happy to help — they just need to be asked.

## 3. Show your work with real photos

Before-and-after photos are especially powerful for trades such as building, painting, landscaping, cleaning and beauty. Take clear photos in good light, ask clients for permission if their home or face is visible, and add the best ones to your business profile and social media.

## 4. Use WhatsApp Status wisely

Your contacts check WhatsApp Status every day. Post a few times a week with:

- A completed job.
- A short tip related to your service.
- Your availability for the coming week.

Keep it useful. Constant sales messages will make people mute you.

## 5. Partner with complementary businesses

Find businesses that serve the same customers but don't compete with you. A plumber and a tiler, a wedding photographer and a florist, or a mechanic and a panel beater can refer customers to each other. Agree on a simple arrangement and keep each other's business cards handy.

## 6. Get involved in your community

Sponsor a prize at a school fundraiser, offer a free workshop at your local library, or support a community clean-up. Local involvement builds recognition and goodwill that money can't easily buy.

## 7. Create helpful content

Answer the questions customers ask you every week. A mechanic could post "Five signs your brakes need attention"; an accountant could explain important tax deadlines. Helpful content positions you as an expert and gives people a reason to follow you.

## 8. Make your contact details impossible to miss

Put your phone number and WhatsApp number on your vehicle, uniform, invoices, quotes and email signature. Every interaction is a chance to be remembered.

## 9. Respond quickly

Speed wins business. Many customers contact two or three providers and choose whoever replies first with a clear answer. Set aside specific times each day to respond to calls, WhatsApps and enquiries, and use quick-reply templates for common questions.

## 10. Offer a simple loyalty incentive

A small discount on the next service, a free add-on or a "refer a friend" thank-you can turn one-off customers into regulars. Keep the offer simple and easy to explain.

## 11. Use local networks

Neighbourhood WhatsApp and community groups can be valuable, but follow each group's rules. Introduce yourself once, then contribute helpfully rather than posting adverts repeatedly.

## 12. Measure what works

Ask every new customer: "How did you hear about us?" Keep a simple tally. On Findora, your dashboard shows how many people viewed your profile and how many called, sent a WhatsApp message or submitted an enquiry. After a few months you'll know exactly where to focus your time.

## Start with three

You don't need to do everything at once. Pick three ideas that suit your business, do them consistently for three months, and review the results. Small, steady efforts compound into a reliable flow of customers.
