A renovation, extension or new build is one of the biggest purchases many households make. Choosing the right builder or contractor matters enormously — a good one delivers quality work on time and on budget, while a poor choice can leave you with delays, defects and disputes.

Use this guide before you appoint anyone.

## Plan the project first

The clearer your plans, the more accurate your quotes. Before contacting builders:

- Decide exactly what you want done and list your must-haves.
- Check whether you need approved building plans from your municipality. Many structural changes, extensions and new structures do.
- Set a realistic budget with a contingency of 10–15% for surprises.

## Shortlist and compare

Find three or more builders who regularly do the type of work you need. Look at their business profiles, photos of completed projects and how long they've been operating. Then request site visits and written quotes from each.

## Questions to ask every builder

### About their business

1. How long have you been operating, and what type of projects do you usually do?
2. Can you show me examples of similar projects — and may I speak to previous clients?
3. Are you registered with the NHBRC? (Builders of new homes are generally required to be.)
4. Do you have insurance that covers your workers and damage to my property?
5. Who will actually do the work — your own team or subcontractors?

### About the quote

6. What exactly is included, and what isn't?
7. Which materials and brands are you pricing for?
8. Who is responsible for rubble removal and clean-up?
9. How long will the job take, and what could cause delays?
10. How do you handle changes I request during the project?

### About payment

11. What deposit do you need, and what is it for?
12. What is the payment schedule, and which milestones trigger each payment?
13. Do you offer a guarantee on workmanship, and for how long?

## Compare quotes carefully

Put the quotes side by side and compare them item by item. A much lower quote may exclude important work, use cheaper materials or underestimate time. Ask each builder to explain any big differences.

## Insist on a written contract

A written agreement protects both you and the builder. It should include:

- The full scope of work and specifications (with plans attached).
- The total price and what it includes.
- A payment schedule linked to completed stages of work.
- Start and completion dates.
- How variations (changes) will be priced and approved — in writing.
- Responsibility for permits, inspections and compliance certificates.
- Guarantee and defects-repair terms.
- How disputes will be resolved.

For large projects, consider having the contract reviewed by an attorney or using a standard building contract.

## Manage payments sensibly

- Pay a reasonable deposit, ideally linked to materials delivered to site.
- Pay the balance in stages as work is completed and inspected.
- Keep a **retention** — a small percentage held back until defects are fixed after completion, if agreed in the contract.
- Pay into a business bank account and keep proof of every payment.

## During the project

- Visit the site regularly and take dated photos.
- Keep communication in writing where possible.
- Approve every change in writing before work starts, including its cost.
- Ask for compliance certificates (electrical, plumbing, gas, glazing) where they apply.

## Red flags

Be careful if a builder:

- Wants most of the money upfront.
- Avoids a written contract.
- Can't show previous work or give references.
- Pushes you to skip building plans or inspections to "save money".
- Is consistently hard to reach before you've even hired them.

## Finishing well

Before the final payment, walk through the project with the builder and list any defects (a "snag list"). Agree on dates to fix them, check the work, and only then make the final payment.

Taking time to choose carefully, documenting everything and paying in stages will dramatically reduce your risk — and make it much more likely that your project is a success.
