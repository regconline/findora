People buy from businesses they trust. That's especially true for services, where customers often can't judge quality until the job is done. Large brands build trust with advertising budgets; small businesses build it through hundreds of small, consistent actions.

Here are practical ways to earn — and keep — your customers' confidence.

## Be transparent about who you are

Customers want to know they're dealing with a real, accountable business:

- Use your actual business name consistently.
- Show where you're based or which areas you serve.
- Introduce the people behind the business — even a short "about us" helps.
- If you're registered with CIPC, a professional body or an industry association, say so (and be ready to show proof).

On Findora you can choose to display your business registration number on your profile. Only claim registrations you actually hold.

## Be clear about prices

Uncertainty about cost is one of the biggest reasons customers hesitate. Where possible:

- Publish "starting from" prices or price ranges for common services.
- Explain call-out or assessment fees upfront.
- Provide written, itemised quotes.
- Tell customers immediately if the scope changes and the price will be affected — before doing the extra work.

## Show real evidence of your work

Photos of real jobs, examples of previous projects and honest descriptions of your experience are more convincing than claims like "best in town". Never use other businesses' photos or invent achievements.

## Communicate reliably

Trust grows when you do what you say:

- Arrive when you said you would, or let the customer know in advance if you'll be late.
- Return calls and messages promptly.
- Explain what you're doing and why.
- Confirm agreements in writing.

Small acts of reliability add up to a reputation.

## Handle mistakes honestly

Every business makes mistakes. What matters is how you respond. Acknowledge the problem, apologise sincerely, fix it properly and learn from it. Customers often become more loyal after seeing a problem handled well.

## Offer a guarantee you can honour

A workmanship guarantee — for example, returning to fix any defect in your work within a set period at no charge — reduces the customer's risk. Make the terms clear and realistic, and always honour them.

## Protect customer information

Customers share names, numbers, addresses and sometimes sensitive details with you. Treat that information with care:

- Use it only to provide the service and communicate about it.
- Don't share it with others without permission.
- Store it securely and delete what you no longer need.
- Don't send marketing messages unless customers have agreed.

Respecting privacy is both a legal obligation under POPIA and a powerful trust signal.

## Make it easy to reach you — before and after the sale

A business that disappears after receiving payment quickly loses trust. Keep the same contact numbers, respond to after-sales questions and be easy to find. A complete, up-to-date directory profile helps customers find you again when they need you.

## Be consistent across every channel

Your profile, WhatsApp, social media, vehicle branding and invoices should all show the same name, logo and contact details. Consistency signals stability and professionalism.

## Avoid shortcuts that damage trust

Some tactics may bring short-term attention but cause long-term harm:

- Fake reviews or testimonials.
- Misleading "verified" or award claims.
- Hidden fees.
- High-pressure sales tactics.
- Copying other businesses' content.

## Trust is built over time

There's no shortcut to a trusted reputation, but every honest quote, punctual arrival and well-handled problem moves you closer. Customers notice — and they tell others.
