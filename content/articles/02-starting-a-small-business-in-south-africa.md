Starting a business is one of the most rewarding things you can do, and South Africa has no shortage of entrepreneurs solving real problems in their communities. The first few months, however, can feel overwhelming. This guide breaks the process into clear steps so you know what to do first, what can wait, and where to get reliable information.

*This article is general guidance, not legal or tax advice. Requirements change, so confirm details with the relevant authority or a qualified professional.*

## Step 1: Test your idea before you spend money

The most expensive mistake new businesses make is building something nobody wants to pay for. Before registering anything:

- **Talk to potential customers.** Ask what they currently do to solve the problem and what frustrates them about it.
- **Check the competition.** Search your area for similar businesses. Competition is not bad — it proves there is demand — but you need a reason for customers to choose you.
- **Make a small first sale.** Offer your service to a few people, even at a discount, and learn from the experience.

## Step 2: Choose a business structure

The most common structures for small businesses are:

- **Sole proprietor.** You trade in your own name or a trading name. It is simple and inexpensive, but there is no legal separation between you and the business.
- **Private company — (Pty) Ltd.** Registered with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC). The company is a separate legal entity, which can make it easier to open accounts, tender for work and bring in partners.
- **Non-profit company (NPC)** for organisations whose purpose is public benefit rather than profit.

Many entrepreneurs start as sole proprietors and register a company once the business grows. An accountant can help you decide which structure suits your situation.

## Step 3: Register with CIPC (if you choose a company)

Company registration is done online through CIPC. You will typically:

1. Reserve a company name (or register with a number-based name).
2. Complete the registration with the details of the directors.
3. Receive your registration certificate and company registration number.

Keep your registration documents safe — banks, clients and suppliers will ask for them. Remember that companies must also file annual returns with CIPC.

## Step 4: Get your tax affairs in order

Every business has tax obligations. Registered companies are generally registered for income tax with the South African Revenue Service (SARS). Sole proprietors declare business income on their personal tax return.

Other registrations may apply depending on your turnover and whether you employ people, for example VAT registration once you reach the compulsory threshold, and PAYE, UIF and SDL when you have employees. A registered tax practitioner can help you understand what applies to you.

## Step 5: Open a separate business bank account

Mixing personal and business money makes it very hard to know whether you are actually making a profit, and it creates headaches at tax time. Open a business account as soon as possible and:

- Pay all business income into it.
- Pay all business expenses from it.
- Pay yourself a regular, fixed amount rather than withdrawing money whenever you need it.

## Step 6: Understand the rules for your industry

Some sectors have specific registration or licensing requirements — for example food businesses, health professionals, financial advisors, security providers, electricians and builders. Find out early whether your trade needs a licence, certificate or professional registration, and budget for it.

## Step 7: Price for profit, not just for sales

Work out what it actually costs you to deliver your product or service, including:

- Materials or stock.
- Transport and fuel.
- Data, airtime and equipment.
- Your own time.

Then add a profit margin. Many new businesses underprice to win customers and end up working hard for very little. It is easier to offer a discount later than to raise prices for existing customers.

## Step 8: Make your business easy to find

Customers can't support a business they can't find. At minimum:

- Create a professional business profile on directories such as Findora, with a clear description, your services and real photos.
- Set up a WhatsApp Business account with your operating hours and a catalogue.
- Ask satisfied customers to recommend you to friends and family.

## Step 9: Keep simple records from day one

Record every sale and expense, keep invoices and receipts, and reconcile your bank account every month. Even a spreadsheet works. Good records help you make decisions, apply for funding and stay compliant.

## Step 10: Look for support

South Africa has many support programmes for small businesses, including government agencies, enterprise development programmes run by larger companies, incubators and local business chambers. Speak to other entrepreneurs in your area too — they often know which programmes are genuinely useful.

## The bottom line

Start small, test your idea, register properly, separate your finances and make it easy for customers to reach you. Every established business you admire started with the same first steps.
