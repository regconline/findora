When someone searches for "plumber near me" or "dentist in Mahikeng", Google often shows a map with a short list of local businesses before any websites. Those listings come from **Google Business Profile** — a free tool that every local business in South Africa should set up.

This guide explains how to create your profile, get it verified and keep it working for you.

## Why your Google Business Profile matters

- **It's free.** There's no cost to create or maintain a profile.
- **It appears where customers look.** Your business can show up on Google Maps and in the local results on Google Search.
- **It answers questions quickly.** Customers see your hours, phone number, directions and photos without visiting a website.
- **It works alongside directories.** Listing on Google *and* on platforms like Findora gives customers more ways to find you, and consistent details across the web build trust.

## Step 1: Check whether a profile already exists

Search for your business name and town on Google Maps. A profile may already exist — created by Google from public information or by a previous owner. If it does, choose **Claim this business** rather than creating a duplicate.

## Step 2: Create your profile

Sign in with a Google account you'll keep long term (ideally a business account, not a staff member's personal email) and add your business. You'll be asked for:

1. **Business name** — your real trading name, exactly as customers know it. Don't add keywords or towns to the name; it can lead to suspension.
2. **Category** — choose the most specific primary category (for example "Dental clinic" rather than "Health").
3. **Location** — add your address if customers visit you. If you visit customers (like a mobile mechanic), you can hide your address and set a service area instead.
4. **Contact details** — phone number and website, if you have one.

## Step 3: Verify your business

Google needs to confirm you're the real owner before your profile goes fully live. Depending on your business, verification may happen by phone, email, video recording or another method Google offers. Follow the instructions carefully and be patient — verification can take a few days.

## Step 4: Complete every section

A complete profile performs better and looks more trustworthy:

- **Hours**, including special hours for public holidays.
- **Services or products**, with short descriptions and prices where appropriate.
- **Business description** — explain what you do, who you serve and where, in plain language.
- **Photos** — your logo, a cover photo, the outside and inside of your premises, your team and examples of your work.
- **Attributes** such as wheelchair accessibility or payment options, where available.

## Step 5: Keep your details consistent everywhere

Search engines compare your business information across websites. Make sure your name, address, phone number and hours match on Google, on your Findora profile, on social media and on your website. Inconsistent details can confuse both customers and search engines.

## Step 6: Encourage genuine reviews

Reviews influence both customers and local rankings. Ask happy customers to leave an honest review, and make it easy by sharing your review link. A few rules:

- Never buy reviews or write fake ones — it breaks Google's policies and can damage your reputation.
- Don't offer rewards in exchange for positive reviews.
- Reply politely to every review, including negative ones. A calm, helpful response shows future customers how you treat people.

## Step 7: Post updates and keep it fresh

You can share updates, offers and events on your profile. Regular activity shows customers you're active. Update your hours immediately when they change — few things frustrate customers more than arriving at a closed business that claims to be open.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Keyword-stuffing your business name.
- Using a virtual office or P.O. Box address where customers can't visit.
- Creating duplicate profiles for the same location.
- Letting an ex-employee keep sole ownership of the profile.

## Bringing it together

Your Google Business Profile and your Findora listing work best together: Google helps people find you in map searches, while Findora lets customers browse by category, compare services and prices, and send you enquiries directly. Set up both, keep them accurate, and review them every few months.
