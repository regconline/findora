Whether your geyser has burst, your car is making a strange noise or you need someone to repaint the house, choosing the right service provider can save you money, time and a great deal of stress. The wrong choice can mean poor workmanship, hidden costs or a job that has to be redone.

This guide gives you a simple, repeatable process you can use for almost any service.

## 1. Be clear about what you need

Before you contact anyone, write down the problem or the job in a few sentences. Include:

- What needs to be done, and where.
- When you need it done, and whether it is urgent.
- Any photos that show the problem.
- Your rough budget, if you have one.

A clear brief helps businesses give you accurate quotes, and it makes it easier to compare them fairly.

## 2. Shortlist at least three businesses

Search for the service in your area and create a shortlist of two or three providers. Look at each business profile carefully:

- **Is the information complete?** Businesses that take the time to describe their services, list operating hours and add real photos of their work are usually more organised.
- **Do they serve your area?** Check the location and service areas so you don't pay unnecessary call-out fees.
- **Do they offer the specific service you need?** A general builder may not be the best choice for specialised waterproofing, for example.

## 3. Check qualifications and registrations

Many trades and professions in South Africa have formal registration requirements. Ask for proof where it applies to your job. For example:

- Electrical work on fixed installations should be done by a registered electrical contractor, who can issue a Certificate of Compliance (CoC) where required.
- Plumbers installing or replacing geysers should be able to provide the relevant plumbing certificate of compliance.
- Health professionals are generally registered with the relevant statutory council, such as the HPCSA.
- Building work on new homes may involve NHBRC requirements for the builder.

You don't need to be an expert in every regulation. Simply asking "Which body are you registered with, and can I see your certificate?" tells you a lot about how a business operates.

## 4. Ask the right questions

When you speak to each business, ask:

1. How long have you been doing this type of work?
2. Can you show me examples of similar jobs?
3. What exactly is included in the price, and what isn't?
4. Is there a call-out or assessment fee?
5. How long will the job take, and when can you start?
6. Do you offer a workmanship guarantee? For how long?
7. How do you prefer to be paid, and when?

Pay attention to how clearly and patiently they answer. Good communication before the job usually means good communication during it.

## 5. Always get a written quote

A verbal estimate is easy to forget or dispute. Ask for a written quote — even a WhatsApp message or email is better than nothing — that lists:

- The work to be done, item by item.
- Materials and their cost.
- Labour cost.
- VAT, if the business is VAT-registered.
- Payment terms and the expected completion date.

Compare quotes line by line rather than only looking at the total. A very low quote can mean lower-quality materials or work that has been left out.

## 6. Be careful with deposits

Some businesses reasonably ask for a deposit to buy materials. Protect yourself by:

- Keeping deposits proportionate to the cost of materials.
- Paying into a business bank account rather than cash where possible.
- Getting a written acknowledgement of every payment.
- Never paying the full amount before the work starts.

If someone pressures you to pay everything upfront "today only", treat it as a warning sign.

## 7. Watch for red flags

Be cautious if a provider:

- Refuses to give a written quote.
- Cannot give a physical address or a consistent contact number.
- Arrives without the tools or materials they said they'd bring.
- Pressures you to make a decision immediately.
- Asks you to pay into a personal account that doesn't match the business name.

## 8. Keep a record of everything

Save quotes, messages, invoices, certificates and photos of the work before and after. If something goes wrong, a clear paper trail makes it much easier to resolve the issue with the business directly or, if necessary, to approach the relevant ombud or the National Consumer Commission.

## Putting it into practice

Choosing a service provider doesn't need to be complicated. Define the job, compare a few well-presented businesses, ask direct questions, get everything in writing and pay sensibly.
