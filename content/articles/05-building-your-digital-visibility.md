"Digital visibility" simply means how easily customers can find your business when they look online. For many small businesses, it is the difference between a phone that rings and one that stays quiet. The good news is that you can build a solid online presence without a big budget or technical skills.

## Start with the foundation: consistent information

Before anything else, decide on your official business details and use them everywhere:

- **Business name** — exactly the same spelling each time.
- **Phone and WhatsApp numbers** — ideally ones that won't change.
- **Address or service area** — where you are based and which areas you cover.
- **Operating hours** — including weekends and public holidays.

Search engines compare information across different websites. When your details match everywhere, they are more confident that your business is real and current. Customers trust consistency too.

## Create your core business profiles

You don't need to be everywhere. Start with a few high-value platforms:

1. **A business directory profile**, such as Findora, with a full description, services, prices where appropriate, photos and contact details. Each approved profile gets its own web page that can appear in search results.
2. **A Google Business Profile**, so you appear on Google Maps and in local search results.
3. **WhatsApp Business**, with a business description, hours, catalogue and quick replies.

Complete every field you can. A half-finished profile suggests a half-finished business.

## Write descriptions customers understand

Describe your business in the words your customers use. Instead of "comprehensive automotive solutions", write "car services, diagnostics, brake repairs and wheel alignment in Klerksdorp". Mention:

- What you do.
- Who you serve.
- Where you operate.
- What makes you different — experience, specialisation, guarantees or response times.

These plain-language descriptions also help search engines understand when to show your business.

## Use real photos

Photos build trust faster than words. Add:

- Your logo and a clear cover photo.
- Your premises, vehicle or workspace.
- Examples of completed work.
- Your team, if they are comfortable with it.

Avoid stock photos that could belong to any business. Customers want to see you.

## Understand the basics of search

You don't need to become an SEO expert, but a few principles help:

- **Relevance** — your profile should clearly describe the services people search for.
- **Location** — mention the towns and suburbs you serve.
- **Completeness** — full profiles generally perform better than sparse ones.
- **Freshness** — update your information when something changes.

Avoid keyword stuffing (repeating the same phrase unnaturally). Write for people first.

## Social media: choose one or two platforms

Pick the platforms your customers actually use. For many local businesses, Facebook and Instagram work well for visual services, while TikTok can suit businesses that can create short, practical videos. LinkedIn is useful for professional and business-to-business services.

Post consistently rather than frequently. Two good posts a week beats ten rushed ones followed by months of silence.

## Make contacting you effortless

Visibility is only valuable if people can reach you easily:

- Add click-to-call and WhatsApp links wherever possible.
- Reply to messages and enquiries quickly.
- Keep your voicemail professional and check it daily.

## Track and improve

Look at the numbers regularly. On Findora, your dashboard shows profile views, phone clicks, WhatsApp clicks, website clicks and enquiries over time. If views are high but contacts are low, improve your description, photos or pricing information. If views are low, check your category, location and service list.

## A simple 30-day plan

- **Week 1:** Decide your official details and complete your Findora and Google profiles.
- **Week 2:** Add at least six good photos and list your main services.
- **Week 3:** Set up WhatsApp Business and share your profile link with existing customers.
- **Week 4:** Review your first results and update anything that's unclear.

Digital visibility is built step by step. Start with the foundation, keep it accurate and improve it gradually.
