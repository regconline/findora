If your business collects names, phone numbers, email addresses or any other information about people, the **Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA)** applies to you. That includes most small businesses — from salons keeping client contact lists to contractors storing quotes and invoices.

POPIA can seem intimidating, but its core idea is simple: **treat people's information responsibly, use it only for legitimate purposes, and keep it safe.**

*This article is a general overview, not legal advice. For your specific situation, consult a qualified professional or the Information Regulator's guidance.*

## Key terms

- **Personal information** — information about an identifiable person, such as a name, ID number, contact details, location, or financial and health information.
- **Responsible party** — the person or business that decides why and how personal information is processed. If you run a business, that's usually you.
- **Data subject** — the person the information is about, such as your customer or employee.
- **Processing** — almost anything you do with information: collecting, storing, using, sharing or deleting it.

## The conditions for lawful processing

POPIA sets out conditions that responsible parties must meet. In practical terms:

1. **Accountability** — your business is responsible for complying.
2. **Processing limitation** — collect only what you need, lawfully, and with a valid reason such as consent or performing a contract.
3. **Purpose specification** — know and explain why you're collecting information.
4. **Further processing limitation** — don't use information for unrelated purposes without a valid basis.
5. **Information quality** — keep information accurate and up to date.
6. **Openness** — tell people what you're collecting and why.
7. **Security safeguards** — protect information against loss, damage and unauthorised access.
8. **Data subject participation** — people can ask to see, correct or delete their information.

## Practical steps for small businesses

### 1. Know what you collect

Make a simple list of the personal information you hold — customer contacts, employee records, supplier details — where it's stored (phone, laptop, cloud, paper) and why you need it.

### 2. Collect only what you need

If you don't need a customer's ID number to provide your service, don't ask for it. Less information means less risk.

### 3. Tell people how you use their information

Have a short privacy notice explaining what you collect, why, who you share it with and how people can contact you. Put it on your website, booking forms or WhatsApp profile.

### 4. Get consent for marketing

Direct marketing by electronic communication (such as SMS, email or WhatsApp) generally requires the person's consent unless they're an existing customer who was given an opportunity to opt out. Every marketing message should identify your business and offer an easy way to unsubscribe.

### 5. Secure your information

- Use strong passwords and two-factor authentication.
- Lock phones and laptops, and keep software updated.
- Restrict access to people who need it.
- Store paper records in a locked cabinet.
- Be careful with shared WhatsApp groups and forwarded messages.

### 6. Share carefully

Only share personal information with others (such as suppliers or contractors) when necessary, and make sure they protect it too.

### 7. Respond to requests

If a customer asks what information you hold about them, or asks you to correct or delete it, respond promptly and reasonably.

### 8. Dispose of information safely

Don't keep information forever. Delete old records you no longer need and shred paper documents.

### 9. Register your Information Officer

Every responsible party has an Information Officer — by default, the head of the business. Businesses are expected to register their Information Officer with the Information Regulator.

### 10. Plan for security incidents

If personal information is lost or accessed without authorisation, POPIA requires you to notify the Information Regulator and the affected people. Know in advance who will handle this.

## Using customer enquiries responsibly

When customers contact you through platforms like Findora, they share their details so you can respond to their enquiry. Use that information to reply and provide your service — don't add them to marketing lists without their permission.

## Final thoughts

POPIA compliance isn't a once-off exercise; it's a habit of respecting people's information. Start with the basics — collect less, explain clearly, secure what you keep and respond to requests — and you'll protect both your customers and your business.
