Power cuts, water interruptions and network outages can bring a small business to a standstill. Every hour you can't serve customers costs money, and frustrated customers may go elsewhere. While you can't control outages, you can reduce their impact with some planning.

## Step 1: Understand your risk

Start by listing what stops working when power, water or internet goes down:

- Card machines, tills and computers.
- Lights, refrigeration, and specialised equipment.
- Wi-Fi and phone signal.
- Water-dependent services (salons, food businesses, cleaning, healthcare).

Then estimate what an hour or a day of downtime costs you. This helps you decide how much to invest in backups.

## Step 2: Prioritise essential equipment

Not everything needs backup power. Identify what's critical to keep trading — usually your router, card machine, a laptop or till, phone charging and a few lights. Powering only essentials is far cheaper than backing up an entire building.

## Step 3: Choose the right backup power

Common options, from smallest to largest:

- **Power banks** for phones and card machines — cheap and essential.
- **Router UPS units** that keep Wi-Fi running for several hours.
- **Inverter and battery systems** for lights, computers and small equipment.
- **Solar with battery storage** for longer outages and reduced electricity bills over time.
- **Generators** for high-power equipment, with running costs, noise and fuel storage to consider.

Use a reputable, qualified installer for any inverter, solar or generator installation, and ask for the relevant compliance certificate. Poor installations can be dangerous and may affect your insurance.

## Step 4: Keep connectivity alive

- Use a router UPS so your internet survives short outages.
- Keep a mobile data backup (a second SIM or a data router from a different network).
- Make sure your card machine can switch to mobile data.

## Step 5: Plan for water interruptions

For water-dependent businesses:

- Store water in clean, food-safe containers or a properly installed tank.
- Know how much water you need per day to keep operating.
- Have a hygiene plan for when water is unavailable, especially in food and health businesses.

## Step 6: Protect your data

- Save files to the cloud rather than only on one computer.
- Enable automatic backups.
- Shut down computers properly when power is low to avoid data corruption.

## Step 7: Communicate with customers

Clear communication keeps customers' trust:

- Update your operating hours on your Findora profile, Google Business Profile and WhatsApp status when outages affect your schedule.
- Let booked customers know early if you need to reschedule.
- Tell customers what still works — for example, "We're open and our card machine works on backup power."

## Step 8: Adjust your schedule

If outages follow a schedule, plan power-intensive work around them. Do your quoting, admin and calls during outages, and equipment-heavy work when power is on.

## Step 9: Review your insurance

Check whether your business insurance covers equipment damage from power surges, spoiled stock or business interruption. Surge protection plugs for sensitive equipment are an inexpensive safeguard.

## Step 10: Start small and build up

You don't need a full solar system on day one. A sensible order:

1. Power banks and surge protection.
2. Router UPS and mobile data backup.
3. Inverter and battery for essentials.
4. Larger solar or generator solutions as the business grows.

## Turning resilience into an advantage

Businesses that stay open and communicate clearly during outages often win customers from competitors who can't. A modest investment in planning can make your business more reliable — and reliability is one of the most valuable things you can offer.
