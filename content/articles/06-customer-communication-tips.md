Two businesses can do equally good work, yet one is always busy while the other struggles. Very often, the difference is communication. Customers remember how you made them feel: whether you replied promptly, explained things clearly and kept your promises.

Here are practical habits that make a real difference.

## Respond quickly — even if it's a short reply

When a customer sends an enquiry, they are often contacting several businesses at once. The first clear, helpful reply has a big advantage.

If you can't give a full answer straight away, acknowledge the message:

> "Thanks for your message, Thandi. I'm on a job until 3pm and will call you this afternoon to discuss your geyser."

That single message keeps the customer from moving on.

## Ask good questions before you quote

Avoid quoting blindly. Ask what you need to know:

- What exactly is the problem or requirement?
- Where is the job, and is there parking or access?
- Is there a deadline?
- Can they send photos?

Good questions show expertise and reduce surprises later.

## Make quotes easy to understand

A clear quote should include:

- A description of the work.
- Itemised materials and labour.
- What is not included.
- Payment terms and validity period.
- Your business details and contact number.

Use plain language. If something is technical, add a sentence explaining why it is needed.

## Set expectations — and then meet them

Tell customers when you'll arrive, how long the job will take and what will happen next. If something changes, tell them before they have to ask. A message such as "We're running 30 minutes late because of traffic on the N12" is far better than an unexplained delay.

## Keep customers updated during longer jobs

For work that takes several days, send short progress updates with a photo. It reassures the customer, reduces anxious phone calls and documents your work.

## Handle complaints calmly

Even excellent businesses get complaints. When it happens:

1. **Listen fully** without interrupting.
2. **Acknowledge** the customer's frustration.
3. **Investigate** the facts.
4. **Offer a fair solution** and agree on next steps.
5. **Follow up** to confirm the issue is resolved.

A complaint handled well can create a more loyal customer than a job with no problems at all.

## Use the right channel

- **Phone calls** are best for urgent or complex discussions.
- **WhatsApp** is convenient for quick updates, photos and confirmations.
- **Email** suits formal quotes, invoices and records.

Whatever you use, confirm important agreements in writing.

## Be professional on WhatsApp

WhatsApp feels casual, but customers still judge professionalism:

- Use a WhatsApp Business account with your business name and hours.
- Greet customers by name and sign off clearly.
- Avoid voice notes for important details such as prices or dates — they're hard to refer back to.
- Respect reasonable hours unless the customer has an emergency.

## Respect privacy

Customers trust you with their names, phone numbers and addresses. Use that information only for the purpose it was shared — to respond to the enquiry and provide the service. Don't add customers to marketing lists without permission, and store records securely. This is good practice and aligns with the Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA).

## Follow up after the job

A short message a few days later — "Just checking that everything is still working well" — shows you care and often leads to referrals. It is also the right moment to ask whether they'd recommend you to others.

## Create a few templates

Save time with ready-made messages for common situations: acknowledging an enquiry, sending a quote, confirming a booking, and following up. Personalise each one with the customer's name and details.

## In summary

Reply fast, ask good questions, quote clearly, keep people informed and follow up. These habits cost nothing, yet they consistently turn enquiries into customers — and customers into ambassadors for your business.
