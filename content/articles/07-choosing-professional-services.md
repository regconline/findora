Hiring a professional — an accountant, attorney, financial advisor, architect or business consultant — is different from hiring a tradesperson. You are paying for expertise and judgement, often on matters that are confidential or have long-term consequences. A good choice can protect you from costly mistakes; a poor one can create them.

Here is how to make a confident decision.

## Define the problem you need solved

"I need an accountant" is a starting point, but be more specific:

- Do you need monthly bookkeeping, annual financial statements, tax returns, or payroll?
- Is your legal matter a property transfer, a labour dispute, a contract review or an estate?
- Are you looking for a once-off project or an ongoing relationship?

Professionals often specialise. A clear description helps you find the right specialist and get a more accurate fee estimate.

## Check registrations and professional membership

Many professions in South Africa are regulated or have recognised professional bodies. Before appointing someone, ask which body they belong to and confirm their status where a public register is available. For example:

- Attorneys are admitted by the High Court and regulated by the Legal Practice Council.
- Tax practitioners must be registered with SARS and a recognised controlling body.
- Accountants may be members of professional bodies such as SAICA, SAIPA or others.
- Financial advisors should be authorised by, or represent a provider authorised by, the Financial Sector Conduct Authority (FSCA).

Membership doesn't guarantee a perfect fit, but it means the professional has met certain standards and is accountable to a regulatory or disciplinary process.

## Ask about relevant experience

Ask how often they handle matters like yours and whether they work with businesses or individuals similar to you. A conveyancer who processes many transfers in your area, or an accountant familiar with your industry, will usually work faster and anticipate issues better.

## Understand how fees work

Professional fees can be structured in different ways:

- **Hourly rates**, common for legal and consulting work.
- **Fixed fees** for clearly defined tasks, such as a tax return or a simple will.
- **Monthly retainers** for ongoing bookkeeping or advisory services.
- **Fees set by tariff or guideline** in certain regulated areas.

Ask for a written fee estimate or engagement letter that explains what is included, how extra work is charged and when payment is due. If a fee seems unusually low or high, ask why.

## Evaluate communication

During your first conversation, notice:

- Do they explain things in language you understand?
- Do they ask thoughtful questions about your situation?
- Do they tell you honestly what they can and cannot do?
- How quickly do they respond?

You will be working closely with this person. Clear communication and mutual respect matter.

## Confidentiality and data protection

Professionals handle sensitive personal and financial information. Ask how they store documents, who in their office will have access, and how they share files with you. Responsible firms take the Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) seriously and will be comfortable answering these questions.

## Watch for warning signs

Be cautious if a professional:

- Guarantees an outcome that depends on a court, regulator or market.
- Pressures you to sign immediately.
- Is vague about fees or refuses to put them in writing.
- Suggests ways to avoid legal obligations that sound too good to be true.
- Cannot confirm their registration.

## Start with a defined first task

If you are unsure, begin with a smaller, clearly defined piece of work. It is a low-risk way to experience how the professional works before committing to a longer relationship.
