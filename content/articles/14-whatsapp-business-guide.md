For many South African customers, WhatsApp is the easiest way to contact a business. It's quick, familiar and works on almost any phone. The free **WhatsApp Business** app adds features designed for businesses, and setting it up properly can make you look more professional and save you hours every week.

## WhatsApp vs WhatsApp Business

The regular WhatsApp app is built for personal chats. WhatsApp Business is a separate free app that adds:

- A **business profile** with your address, hours, description, email and website.
- A **catalogue** to showcase products or services.
- **Quick replies** for answers you type often.
- **Greeting and away messages** sent automatically.
- **Labels** to organise chats (for example "New enquiry", "Quoted", "Paid").

Ideally, use a separate number for your business so personal and work conversations don't mix.

## Step 1: Complete your business profile

Fill in every field:

- **Business name** — the same name customers see on your signage and your Findora listing.
- **Category** — the closest match to what you do.
- **Description** — one or two sentences on what you offer and where.
- **Address and hours** — so customers know when to expect a reply.
- **Website or profile link** — you can link to your Findora business profile if you don't have a website.

Use your logo as the profile picture so customers instantly recognise you.

## Step 2: Build a simple catalogue

Add your main services or products with a photo, a short description and a price where possible. Customers can browse the catalogue before they message you, which means fewer repetitive questions and better-prepared enquiries.

## Step 3: Set up greeting and away messages

A **greeting message** welcomes new customers, for example:

> "Hi! Thanks for contacting Mokoena Plumbing. Please tell us your area and describe the problem — a photo helps. We reply within an hour during working hours."

An **away message** sets expectations after hours:

> "Thanks for your message. We're closed now and will reply from 7:30 tomorrow morning. For burst pipes, call 082 000 0000."

## Step 4: Create quick replies

Save answers to the questions you get most — prices, directions, payment options, booking information. Type "/" in a chat to insert them. Personalise each one with the customer's name before sending.

## Step 5: Organise chats with labels

Labels turn WhatsApp into a simple sales pipeline. A useful set:

- New enquiry
- Quote sent
- Booked
- Awaiting payment
- Completed

At a glance you'll know who needs a follow-up.

## Step 6: Share your WhatsApp link

WhatsApp lets you create a click-to-chat link. Put it on your social media, email signature and business cards. On Findora, adding your WhatsApp number shows a **WhatsApp** button on your profile so customers can message you in one tap — and your dashboard shows how many people used it.

## Good WhatsApp etiquette

- **Respect hours.** Avoid messaging customers late at night unless they asked you to.
- **Use text for important details.** Voice notes are convenient, but prices, dates and addresses should be written so they can be found later.
- **Don't add people to broadcast lists without permission.** Marketing messages require consent, and unwanted messages can get your number reported.
- **Keep it professional.** Friendly is good; slang and excessive emojis can undermine trust in some industries.
- **Protect personal information.** Don't forward customer details to others without a good reason.

## Measure what's working

Keep track of how many chats turn into paying jobs and how quickly you respond. Many businesses find that simply replying faster increases their conversion rate. Combine this with the contact statistics in your Findora dashboard to see which channels bring in the most customers.

## Final tip

WhatsApp Business is most powerful when it's part of a complete presence: a well-written directory profile, accurate Google listing and quick, friendly replies. Set it up once, keep it tidy, and it will quietly become one of your most valuable business tools.
