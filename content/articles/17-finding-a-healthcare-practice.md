Whether you've moved to a new town, need a specialist or simply want a practice that suits your family better, choosing a healthcare provider is an important decision. Unlike most services, healthcare involves trust, privacy and continuity of care. Here's a practical way to approach it.

*This article is general information, not medical advice. In an emergency, go to your nearest emergency unit or call emergency services immediately.*

## Start with what you need

Different needs call for different providers:

- **General practitioners (GPs)** are usually the first point of contact for everyday health concerns, chronic condition management and referrals.
- **Dentists, optometrists and physiotherapists** provide specialised primary care in their fields.
- **Specialists** (such as paediatricians, gynaecologists or surgeons) often require a referral, especially if you want your medical aid to cover the consultation.
- **Pharmacies, nursing services and allied health practitioners** support ongoing care.

Knowing which type of provider you need narrows your search and avoids unnecessary visits.

## Confirm professional registration

Healthcare practitioners in South Africa must be registered with the relevant statutory body — most with the **Health Professions Council of South Africa (HPCSA)**, nurses with the **South African Nursing Council**, and pharmacists with the **South African Pharmacy Council**. These councils offer ways to check a practitioner's registration status. It's a quick, sensible step, especially before seeing someone for the first time.

## Check medical aid and payment options

If you have medical aid:

- Check whether the practice is in your scheme's network, if your plan uses one — out-of-network visits may cost more or not be covered.
- Ask whether the practice charges scheme rates or more, and whether you'll need to pay upfront and claim back.
- For specialists, confirm whether you need a referral.

If you're paying cash, ask for the consultation fee upfront. Many practices are happy to tell you.

## Consider location and hours

Practical factors matter, especially for families and chronic care:

- How far is the practice from home or work?
- Are the hours suitable — early mornings, evenings or Saturdays?
- Is there parking, and is the building accessible?
- How do they handle urgent same-day appointments?

Business profiles that list operating hours and locations make this easy to compare.

## Look at the services offered

Many practices offer more than consultations — for example chronic medication management, vaccinations, wellness screenings, minor procedures, family planning, or telehealth consultations. Choosing a practice that covers most of your needs can make care more convenient and coordinated.

## Notice how they communicate

Your first contact says a lot:

- Is the reception team helpful and polite?
- Do they answer questions about fees and appointments clearly?
- Is it easy to book — by phone, WhatsApp or online?
- Do they explain how they protect your medical information?

Good communication is a strong sign of a well-run practice.

## Privacy and your health information

Health information is special personal information under POPIA and must be treated with extra care. A responsible practice will:

- Keep your records confidential and secure.
- Share information with others (such as specialists or medical aids) only as allowed by law or with your consent.
- Let you access your own records on request.

## Continuity of care matters

Seeing the same GP or practice over time means they understand your medical history, which helps with prevention and long-term conditions. If you find a provider you trust, keeping your care with them has real benefits.
