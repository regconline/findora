Most service providers are honest people trying to earn a living. Unfortunately, a minority of scammers take advantage of customers who need help quickly. Knowing the common tactics makes it much harder for them to succeed.

## Common scams to watch for

### The upfront payment scam

A "business" offers an unusually good price, then asks for full or large upfront payment — for materials, a "booking fee" or a "special discount that ends today". Once paid, they stop responding.

**Protect yourself:** Pay deposits only in proportion to genuine material costs, ideally directly to a supplier or into a business account that matches the business name. Never pay the full amount before work begins.

### Changed banking details

You receive an email or message, apparently from a business you've dealt with, saying their bank details have changed. The new account belongs to a fraudster.

**Protect yourself:** Always confirm changed banking details by phoning the business on a number you already know — not one supplied in the message.

### The fake listing or copied identity

Scammers copy the name, logo or photos of a real business and advertise under it with a different phone number.

**Protect yourself:** Check that contact details match across the business's profiles. Be cautious if a business's photos look like stock images or appear under different business names. ### The "unnecessary repairs" tactic

A provider inspects your geyser, car or roof and claims that extensive, urgent repairs are needed — far beyond the original problem.

**Protect yourself:** For large or unexpected repairs, ask for a written explanation and photos, then get a second quote before agreeing.

### Unrequested door-to-door services

Someone arrives unannounced offering to fix a problem they claim to have noticed, often demanding payment on the spot.

**Protect yourself:** Don't let unexpected visitors into your home. Take their details and verify the business independently before agreeing to anything.

### Overpayment and refund requests

A "customer" or "supplier" pays you more than agreed and asks you to refund the difference. The original payment later reverses.

**Protect yourself:** Wait for funds to clear fully in your account before refunding anything, and be suspicious of overpayments.

## General warning signs

Be cautious when someone:

- Pressures you to decide or pay immediately.
- Offers a price far below other quotes.
- Avoids giving a written quote, address or registration details.
- Insists on cash or untraceable payment methods.
- Uses a personal bank account in a different name.
- Gives inconsistent information about their business.

## Good habits that protect you

1. **Get at least two quotes** for significant work.
2. **Ask for proof** of relevant registrations and certificates.
3. **Get everything in writing** — quote, scope, timeline, payment terms.
4. **Keep records** of messages, invoices and payments.
5. **Pay in stages** linked to completed work.
6. **Trust your instincts.** If something feels wrong, pause.

## If something goes wrong

- Stop any further payments immediately.
- Contact your bank as soon as possible if you paid electronically.
- Report fraud to the South African Police Service and obtain a case number.
- Report the listing on the platform where you found the business — on Findora, use "Report this listing" on the business profile.
- For disputes with legitimate businesses, consider the relevant industry ombud or the National Consumer Commission.

## Staying safe while supporting local business

Scams shouldn't stop you from supporting local businesses. By comparing profiles, asking the right questions and paying sensibly, you can enjoy the benefits of local services while keeping your money safe.
